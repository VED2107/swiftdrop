Three steps. Then it just works.

No account, no app store, no cables. Your phone uses its normal browser.

  1. 1

    Open SwiftDrop on your PC

    Run SwiftDrop.exe. Its window shows a code to scan.

  2. 2

    Scan it with your phone

    Point your iPhone or Android camera at the code, then tap Allow on the PC. Same Wi-Fi, or your phone's hotspot.

  3. 3

    Send anything, either way

    Pick photos on the phone and they land in a folder on your PC. Drop files on the PC and they're ready on the phone.

Nothing leaves your network

Files travel from one device to the other over your own Wi-Fi. There's no upload, no server in between, and it works without internet.

As fast as your Wi-Fi

Transfers run over several parallel connections that adapt to your network, so big videos and thousand-photo batches move at the speed your router allows.

Picks up where it stopped

Lose Wi-Fi halfway through a 10 GB video? SwiftDrop resumes from the last piece, never from zero, and checks every piece arrived intact.

Straight into your folder

No "download each file" prompts on the PC. Everything lands in the folder you choose, with folders kept intact.

Private by default.

SwiftDrop never sees your files. There's no SwiftDrop cloud to see them. A new device can only connect after you tap Allow on your PC, and pairing codes expire in minutes.

Questions

Do I need to install anything on my phone?

No. SwiftDrop opens in Safari or Chrome on your phone after you scan the code. Only the PC runs the app.

Does it work with Android?

Yes. iPhone and Android both work: scan with the camera (or Google Lens), or type the 6-character code shown on the PC.

Do I need internet?

No. Both devices only need to be on the same Wi-Fi. If there's no Wi-Fi, turn on your phone's hotspot and connect the PC to it.

Windows asked about the firewall. Is that normal?

Yes. The first time SwiftDrop starts, Windows asks whether it may accept connections. Allow it on private networks so your phone can reach your PC.

Are there size limits?

Not in practice. Large videos stream straight to disk on the PC. Keep SwiftDrop on screen on your phone while it sends, since phones pause web pages in the background.

Will my iPhone photos stay HEIC?

The iPhone photo picker may convert to JPEG. For originals, tap Options → Current in the picker, or send them from the Files app.
